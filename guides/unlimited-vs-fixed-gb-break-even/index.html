Break-even math

Unlimited or Fixed-GB: Which eSIM Is Cheaper for Your Trip?

"Unlimited" is the most expensive word in travel data. It is also the right answer for some people. Here is the threshold that separates them — and the hotspot cap that flips it back.

The short answer: at roughly $15 for 10 GB — about $1.50/GB — that is your reference price for fixed data. A 7-day unlimited plan at $22.00 (Nomad) breaks even once you would have used more than 14.7 GB. Below that, fixed-GB is cheaper. Above it, unlimited wins.

The rule of thumb

Most travellers use far less data than they assume. Mapping, messaging and photos are cheap. Video is not. A 7-day trip with normal use is typically 3–8 GB. A week of video calls, cloud backups and tethering can be 20 GB or more.

So the break-even sits around 20 GB/week — roughly 2.85 GB per day. If you are below that, buy fixed-GB. If you are above it, unlimited is cheaper and you stop thinking about it.

"Unlimited" is not unlimited

Every unlimited plan in the market has a fair-use policy, and the policies differ in a way that matters enormously. Read the two columns that decide it:

  • High-speed allowance per day. Once you burn it, you are throttled to something like 1 Mbps. That is fine for email and messaging, painful for video calls and unusable for a laptop hotspot.
  • Hotspot tethering cap. Some vendors cap tethering at 1 GB/day; others allow uncapped hotspot on unlimited plans. If you plan to run a laptop off your phone, a 1 GB/day cap makes unlimited a false economy — the plan is "unlimited" for the phone and useless for the laptop.

The cheapest unlimited plan is the dearest one if your actual use is heavy tethering. This is exactly why the calculator asks whether you are using a laptop hotspot — it changes the recommendation, not just the price.

The decision

Buy fixed-GB if…

  • You use under ~2.85 GB/day
  • You mostly need maps, messaging and photos
  • You are tethering a laptop heavily
  • You want a hard cap and no throttling surprises

Buy unlimited if…

  • You exceed ~2.85 GB/day
  • You stream video or do video calls
  • You travel somewhere with unreliable Wi-Fi
  • You want zero thinking on day 6 of the trip
One more thing. The unlimited price you see in a benchmark is the list price for a specific destination. Regional plans, promotional pricing and app-only discounts all move this number around, which is why the break-even has to be recalculated per trip rather than memorised once.
